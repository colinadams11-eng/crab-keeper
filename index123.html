Crab care guide

Why is my crab digging so much?

Digging is normal. Crabs dig to chill out, cool down, get to moist sand, or molt. If one stays buried for days, it's probably molting, so leave it alone. Keep the substrate at least 3 times as deep as your biggest crab is tall (about 6 inches is great).

Is my humidity okay?

Aim for 70–80%. Too low: mist the sand and coconut fiber with dechlorinated water and cover part of the lid with plastic wrap to hold moisture in. Too high (water beading on the glass, mold): crack the lid open a little for airflow.

Meal ideas

Mix it up every day: a protein (boiled egg, plain shrimp, plain chicken), a fruit (mango, banana, coconut), a veggie (carrot, spinach, sweet potato), and calcium (cuttlebone, crushed eggshell). Tiny portions, and toss leftovers every day.

Signs a crab is sick

Sitting above ground and barely moving for days, dropping legs, hanging out of its shell, or not eating for a long time while above ground. Check temp and humidity first, since those cause most problems. A strong rotten smell can mean a crab has died. Don't confuse a molted skin (looks like a whole crab but is hollow) with a dead crab.

Do they like their shells?

Keep 3+ spare shells per crab with openings a little bigger than their current ones. Purple pinchers like round openings (turbo shells). Boil new shells in dechlorinated water and let them cool first. If they fight over shells or keep swapping, add more options.

Spare shells
